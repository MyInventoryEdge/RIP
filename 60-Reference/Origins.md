# Origins

## The Grocery Cart

In the mid-1990s, Bob Berry founded The Grocery Cart in Des Moines, Iowa. Operating through `gcart.com`, the company pursued online grocery ordering and delivery at a time when dial-up connections, early web browsers, and public uncertainty about electronic commerce defined the environment.

Hy-Vee Food Stores supported the effort and provided access to product data. Where complete electronic product information did not yet exist, missing items were manually scanned and assembled.

The company did not achieve financial success. It produced something else of lasting value: hard-earned knowledge about timing, systems, integration, customer readiness, operational execution, and informed risk.

The Grocery Cart is recognized here not as a concealed failure or a polished success story, but as a major teacher in the history that eventually contributed to RIP.

Its lesson is foundational:

> We honor informed risk. We preserve hard-earned lessons. We measure progress not only by success, but by the knowledge we refuse to lose.

RIP does not exist to make organizations incapable of failure. It exists so they do not lose what experience taught them or pay repeatedly for the same lesson.
