# RIP-000 — Constitution

**Status:** Approved Foundational Authority  
**Authority:** Repository Intelligence Platform Gatekeeper  
**Purpose:** Define the foundational authority, obligations, and boundaries of RIP.

## 1. Mission

RIP SHALL preserve, govern, apply, and continuously evolve organizational knowledge through explicit authority, traceable provenance, governed learning, and validated implementation.

## 2. Primary Object

The Organization SHALL be RIP's primary object.

Repositories, products, services, people, infrastructure, operations, providers, Hosts, decisions, and history SHALL be modeled as parts of or relationships within the organization.

No implementation technology or repository SHALL silently replace the Organization as the center of the model.

## 3. Authority

The organization is the sole source of its authority.

No Host, model, tool, repository, provider, person acting outside delegated authority, or automated process may silently create organizational policy.

Authority exists only when recognized through the organization's approved governance process.

## 4. Knowledge

RIP SHALL preserve organizational knowledge with sufficient context to understand:

- what is believed or known;
- where it came from;
- what evidence supports it;
- whether it is supplied, inferred, proposed, approved, rejected, deferred, superseded, or historical;
- who or what created it;
- who authorized it;
- how it was implemented;
- whether it was validated;
- what later evidence changed it.

Knowledge without provenance SHALL NOT be represented as trusted organizational knowledge.

## 5. Conversation and Institutional Memory

A conversation is not institutional memory.

Important organizational understanding SHALL be promoted from transient conversation into durable governed artifacts.

RIP SHALL support the lifecycle:

```text
Conversation
    ↓
Idea
    ↓
Proposal
    ↓
Decision
    ↓
Implementation
    ↓
Validation
    ↓
Institutional Knowledge
```

## 6. Hosts

A Host is a constitutional executor.

A Host SHALL:

- interpret and enact approved organizational authority;
- identify when required authority or context is missing;
- distinguish supplied knowledge, inferred knowledge, recommendations, and authority;
- refuse to represent an unapproved preference as policy;
- preserve traceability between actions, evidence, knowledge, and authority;
- expose uncertainty rather than conceal it;
- operate within explicit capability boundaries.

A Host SHALL NOT:

- silently create constitutional authority;
- overwrite organizational history;
- treat generated output as inherently authoritative;
- bypass the Gatekeeper;
- define the organization's conceptual architecture through provider-specific assumptions.

## 7. Learning and Governance

RIP separates learning from governance.

The learning system MAY observe evidence, infer technical knowledge, recognize patterns, identify inconsistencies, and generate ideas or proposals.

Only authorized governance MAY convert a proposal into organizational authority.

The system learns. The organization governs.

## 8. Institutional Memory

RIP SHALL preserve:

- ideas of lasting organizational relevance;
- proposals;
- evidence;
- decisions;
- decision rationale;
- rejected and deferred alternatives;
- implementation outcomes;
- validation results;
- reconsideration criteria;
- provenance;
- relevant historical context.

Rejected, deferred, withdrawn, and superseded artifacts SHALL remain part of the institutional record.

## 9. Concepts and Providers

RIP SHALL model durable concepts independently from provider-specific implementations.

Providers SHALL implement declared concepts and capabilities.

Provider selection SHALL NOT redefine the organization's conceptual model without governed authorization.

## 10. Operations and Changes

Operations SHALL be derived from current organizational understanding, authority, dependencies, and evidence.

Changes SHALL be traceable from origin through proposal, authority, implementation, operational effect, and validation.

## 11. Evolution

Organizations SHALL preserve their history without becoming prisoners of past decisions.

Past decisions are evidence and history, not immutable law. Authority may evolve through the governed proposal process.

## 12. Constitutional Purpose

Every directory, artifact, protocol, field, interface, relationship, and automated behavior in RIP SHALL have an identifiable constitutional purpose.

Artifacts without a defensible purpose SHOULD be removed, deferred, rejected, or clearly marked as experimental.

## 13. Deliverability

RIP SHALL be built as a usable system rather than an aspirational description.

Foundational artifacts SHALL be:

- inspectable;
- traceable;
- understandable;
- versioned;
- implementable;
- transferable;
- internally consistent;
- capable of validation.

## 14. Self-Governance

RIP SHALL apply this Constitution to its own evolution.

Its repository SHOULD NOT intentionally lag behind approved organizational understanding.

When a coherent body of decisions has been governed, the repository SHOULD be brought into alignment in a complete consistency pass rather than fragmented into artificial documentation phases.

## 15. Amendment

This Constitution may be amended only through an approved RIP proposal. Every amendment SHALL retain its proposal history, decision rationale, implementation record, and validation result.
